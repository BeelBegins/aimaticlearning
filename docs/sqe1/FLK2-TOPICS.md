# FLK2 coverage checklist

From the SRA FLK2 PDF applicable from 1 September 2026 (April 2026 review).
Official file: [official/sqe1-flk2-from-september-2026.pdf](official/sqe1-flk2-from-september-2026.pdf).

Assessment objectives on the live FLK2 page are summarised under each subject.
Ethics can wrap any heading. Taxation only under Property and Wills.
Solicitors Accounts is sampled in **both** sessions (wills vs conveyancing).

Candidates will not be required to demonstrate foreign law, foreign assets, or
foreign taxes (Wills and Trusts AOs).

## Wills and the Administration of Estates — Session 1

Blueprint area includes Solicitors Accounts in this context.

Assessment objectives: validity and interpretation of a will; testate, intestate
and partial intestacy; grants; IHT on lifetime gifts and death; planning and
progressing administration including 1975 Act claims; PRs and trustees;
beneficiaries’ rights, powers and remedies.

### Wills and intestacy

- Validity of wills and codicils: testamentary capacity; knowledge and approval; duress and undue influence; formal requirements; who has the burden of proof when challenging validity
- Personal representatives: appointment of executors (eligibility, suitability); renunciation; reservation of power
- Alterations before and after execution; use and effect of codicils
- Revocation: later will; codicil; destruction; effect of marriage and civil partnership; effect of divorce or dissolution
- Interpretation: types of gift; failure of gifts
- Intestacy: Administration of Estates Act 1925 s.46; statutory trusts
- Property passing outside the estate: joint property; life policies; pension scheme benefits; trust property

### Probate and administration

- Need for a grant
- Non-Contentious Probate Rules: order of priority with a will; on intestacy
- Applications: probate; letters of administration with will annexed; letters of administration; evidence required
- Valuation of assets and liabilities; excepted estates
- Funding the initial IHT payment
- Burden, liability and incidence of IHT: personal representatives, beneficiaries, trustees, co-owners
- PR duties before distribution: collect and secure; preserve value; collect money due; issue proceedings; pay funeral and testamentary expenses; follow will or intestacy; account to beneficiaries
- PR liabilities and protection: unknown, missing, or insolvent beneficiaries; creditors
- Sale of assets to pay funeral expenses, tax, debts and legacies; distribution
- Inheritance (Provision for Family and Dependants) Act 1975: time limit, applicants, ground and factors the court considers

### Taxation — wills and estates

- IHT: immediately chargeable lifetime transfers; PETs; gifts with reservation of benefit; transfers on death; exemptions and reliefs; anti-avoidance
- Income Tax and CGT during administration: PRs’ liability; beneficiaries’ CGT on inherited assets

## Trusts Law — Session 1

Assessment objectives: express and implied trusts; fiduciary relationship;
trustees’ duties, powers and liability; equitable remedies.

- Express trusts: three certainties (fixed and discretionary); formalities; constitution; exceptions to “equity will not assist a volunteer” including *Re Rose*, *Strong v Bird*, *Choithram v Pagarani*
- Beneficial entitlement: fixed, discretionary, vested, contingent; *Saunders v Vautier*
- Charitable trusts vs non-charitable purpose trusts
- Resulting trusts: automatic; presumed (when presumed and when not)
- Trusts of the family home: common intention constructive trust (joint or sole legal title; express agreement; direct and indirect contributions); proprietary estoppel
- Liability of strangers: knowing receipt; dishonest assistance
- Fiduciary obligations: no profit; no purchase of trust property; no conflict
- Trustees: who can be one; appointment, removal, retirement; duty of care; duty and powers of investment; statutory maintenance and advancement
- Trustees’ liability: breach; measure; protection; limitation
- Equitable remedies and tracing in equity

## Land Law — Session 1

Assessment objectives: registered and unregistered land; freehold and leasehold
estates; legal and equitable interests; landlord and tenant; co-ownership.

- Nature of land: real vs personal property; acquiring/transferring legal estates; legal and equitable interests; protecting and enforcing third-party interests; how land can be held; formalities
- Registered title: estates that can be substantively registered; protecting interests; overriding interests vs interests that must be protected
- Unregistered title: title deeds; Land Charges; doctrine of notice
- Co-ownership: joint tenants vs tenants in common (law and equity); survivorship; severance; TLATA 1996 ss.14–15
- Proprietary rights: easements (characteristics and creation); freehold covenants (benefit and burden); mortgages (enforceability, priority, lender’s powers and duties, protection of mortgagors and other third parties)
- Leases: landlord and tenant; lease vs licence; privity of contract and estate; passing of leasehold covenants; alienation covenants; remedies including forfeiture; termination

## Solicitors Accounts — Session 1 (wills context) and Session 2 (property context)

Assessment objectives: client money vs firm money; ledgers and bank accounts;
interest; breaches; accounting entries, bills, accountants’ reports, records.

Blueprint **folds** this into Wills (session 1) and Property (session 2). Tag
the primary area as Wills or Property according to the fact pattern; still
cover every heading below across the three mocks.

- Client money: definition; pay into client account; when it may be withheld; repayment; entries
- Client account: meaning and name; not a banking facility; withdrawals and entries
- Keep client money separate from money of the authorised body
- Interest on client money and entries
- Correct breaches promptly on discovery; entries
- Accurate records and reconciliation; record of bills
  - disbursements: agency and principal methods
  - transfers
  - submission, reduction and payment of bills including VAT
  - accounting entries
- Joint accounts; client’s own account
- Third-party managed accounts
- Accountants’ reports; storage and retention of accounting records

## Property Law and Practice — Session 2

Blueprint area includes Solicitors Accounts in conveyancing.

Assessment objectives: key elements of freehold/leasehold residential or
commercial transactions; investigating registered or unregistered title;
pre-contract searches and enquiries; steps to exchange; pre-completion;
completion and post-completion in England **and** Wales including delayed-
completion remedies; grant and assignment of a commercial lease/underlease;
key commercial lease covenants and breach; LTA 1954 Part II; property tax
(SDLT / LTT, VAT, CGT).

### Freehold

- Structure of freehold transactions
- Investigating registered title (official copies) and unregistered title (epitome; deduce ownership)
- Issues from investigation and further action; reporting to the client
- Searches and enquiries: range and purpose; who raises them; issues from results
- Law Society Conveyancing Protocol
- Finance: sources; types of mortgage
- Acting for a lender: requirements; certificate of title
- Exchange: drafting the sale contract; Standard Conditions of Sale; Standard Commercial Property Conditions; special conditions
- Deposits: stakeholder vs agent; insurance and risk; VAT in a contract
- Timing of certificate of title to lender
- Practice, method and authority to exchange (**Law Society formulae**); consequences of exchange
- Pre-completion: transfer deed and execution formalities; searches; steps including financial considerations and apportionments
- Completion methods and effect; post-completion
- Delayed completion: common law damages; contractual compensation; notice to complete; rescission

### Leasehold

- Structure and content: repair, insurance, alterations, user and planning, rent and rent review, alienation, term including break clauses, Code for Leasing Business Premises
- Grant of lease or underlease: drafting; agreement for lease; deduce and investigate title; searches; pre-completion; completion and post-completion
- Assignment: drafting the contract for assignment; title; searches; landlord’s consent; deed of assignment/transfer and covenants for title; pre-completion; authorised guarantee agreement; completion and post-completion
- Licence to assign / underlet: purpose, who drafts, privity, key provisions
- Liability on covenants: leases granted before 1 January 1996 vs on or after
- Breach remedies: debt; forfeiture; CRAR; guarantors and rent deposit; specific performance; damages; self-help / *Jervis v Harris*
- Termination: effluxion of time; surrender; merger
- LTA 1954 Part II: application including contracting out; tenant renewal; landlord termination; grounds of opposition; terms of new lease; compensation

### Planning (England and Wales)

- Statutory definition of “Development”; what is and is not development; what does not need express permission
- Building regulations
- Enforcement: time limits and LPA powers

### Taxation — property

- SDLT (England) and LTT (Wales): residential and non-residential freehold
- VAT: taxable supply; standard / exempt / zero-rated; option to tax and effect
- CGT: basis of charge; Private Residence Relief

## Criminal Liability — Session 2

Assessment objective: core principles of criminal liability (actus reus, mens
rea, specific and general defences, participation) **in the specified offences
only**. Do not examine offences outside this list.

Specified offences:

- Offences against the person: common assault (assault and battery); OAPA 1861 ss.47, 20, 18
- Theft Act 1968 ss.1, 8, 9, 10
- Criminal damage: simple, aggravated, arson
- Homicide: murder; voluntary manslaughter; involuntary (unlawful act; gross negligence)
- Fraud: false representation; abuse of position; failing to disclose

Also:

- Actus reus and mens rea of those offences
- General defences: intoxication; self-defence / defence of another
- Partial defences: loss of control; diminished responsibility
- Parties: principal; accomplices
- Inchoate: attempt

## Criminal Law and Practice — Session 2

Assessment objectives: police station; pre-trial; meeting the client’s
objectives; magistrates’ and Crown Court trial including sentencing and
appeals. Includes advising **vulnerable** clients.

- Police station
  - detention rights: legal advice; have someone informed; PACE 1984 Code C reviews and time limits
  - identification: when required; types; Code D procedure
  - silence and adverse inferences
  - interviews: defence solicitor conduct including vulnerable clients; appropriate adult (who can be one); requirements of a lawful interview under Code C
- Bail: right and exceptions; conditional bail; applying for and opposing; further applications; appeals; absconding and breaches
- First hearing: classification; representation order; what happens; defence solicitor’s role
- Plea before venue; advising on trial venue
- Allocation: MCA 1980 ss.19–20 and s.22A; sending without allocation CDA 1998 ss.50A and 51
- Case management: magistrates’ directions; PTPH; disclosure (prosecution, defence, unused)
- Evidence
  - burden and standard of proof
  - visual identification and Turnbull
  - inferences from silence CJPOA 1994 ss.34–38
  - hearsay: definition, grounds, challenging admissibility
  - confessions: definition, admissibility, ss.76 and 78 PACE
  - bad character: definition; CJA 2003 s.101(1) seven gateways; s.100(1) three gateways; procedure; court’s power to exclude
  - exclusion under s.78 PACE and the right to a fair trial
- Trial (magistrates’ and Crown Court)
  - stages including no case to answer
  - modes of address, etiquette, leading vs non-leading
  - competence and compellability; special measures
  - relevance of defendant’s **good** character
  - solicitor’s duty to the court
- Sentencing: guidelines; seriousness (aggravating/mitigating); concurrent/consecutive; mitigation; custodial, suspended, community; Newton hearings (applicability and procedure)
- Appeals
  - magistrates’: conviction/sentence; Crown Court powers; case stated to High Court
  - Crown Court: grounds, procedure, Court of Appeal powers
- Youth court: jurisdiction and grave crimes; allocation; youths jointly charged with an adult; Sentencing Children and Young People guidelines; Robbery — Sentencing Children and Young People guideline; referral orders; detention and training orders; youth rehabilitation orders
- Welsh language in criminal proceedings
